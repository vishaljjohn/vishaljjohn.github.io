What is Bluegrass Biodesign

Bluegrass Biodesign is a nine-month interdisciplinary innovation program at the University of Louisville that trains the next generation of medical device developers. Students from the School of Medicine, College of Business, and J.B. Speed School of Engineering work in multidisciplinary teams to identify unmet clinical needs, design solutions, prototype devices, and validate them against real-world constraints.

The program is grounded in the Stanford Biodesign framework — a structured methodology moving from clinical need-finding through prototyping and market validation. All teams complete business training through UofL's NSF-funded I-Corps program, receiving funding and mentorship to bring their ideas to life.

Our Device: ATriFlow

Heart attacks, strokes, and heart failure flare-ups (together called major adverse cardiovascular events, or MACE) often build silently between clinic visits. The early warning signs are changes in the blood vessels themselves: arteries stiffening, blood flow to the legs dropping, and the nervous system's control of the heart slipping. Today those can only be measured in a clinic, one test at a time, with blood pressure cuffs, Doppler ultrasound, or ECG equipment and a trained operator. So most patients get a snapshot a few times a year, and changes in between are missed.

ArmWristAnkle

ATriFlow is a clinician-prescribed wearable made of three soft bands, worn on the arm, wrist, and ankle. Each band uses light-based pulse sensors (photoplethysmography, the same kind of sensor in a smartwatch), and together they track four markers of vascular health without a single blood pressure cuff. A patient app guides setup and checks signal quality, and a physician dashboard shows trends over time and flags changes from a patient's baseline, so care teams can step in before a crisis instead of after.

We built a working first-generation prototype that measures all four markers and tested it in controlled sessions with healthy volunteers. Next steps include testing over longer periods and during everyday activity.

What it tracks

ABIAnkle-brachial indexCompares blood flow at the ankle and the arm. A drop can signal peripheral artery disease.
baPWVPulse wave velocityHow fast the pulse travels from arm to ankle. Faster means stiffer arteries.
RIReflection indexHow strongly the pulse wave bounces back, a window into vessel tone and endothelial health.
HRVHeart rate variabilityBeat-to-beat variation, which reflects how well the nervous system is regulating the heart.

The Team

Our team came from the School of Medicine and the J.B. Speed School of Engineering, mentored by Dr. Shahab Ghafghazi, MD (cardiology) and Dr. Guruprasad Giridharan, PhD (bioengineering). I worked with medical students Ellen Oskoui, Amos Flomo, Caleb He, and Nikita Nair and bioengineering student Swetha Senthil-Nathan. Prototyping involved everything from late-night sessions with circuit boards to using each other as test subjects, all in the spirit of actually making something that works.

Technical details of the design are confidential while the University of Louisville evaluates the invention.
